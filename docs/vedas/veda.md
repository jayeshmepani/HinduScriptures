# Comprehensive Architecture, Taxonomy, and Inventory of the Vedic and Post-Vedic Literary Corpus

The Sanskrit Vedic and post-Vedic literary corpus—together with its theological, philosophical, scientific, and ritual extensions—is organized across multiple intersecting axes rather than a single flat list of equivalent categories. The terms **Veda** (वेद), **Saṃhitā** (संहिता), **Brāhmaṇa** (ब्राह्मण), **Āraṇyaka** (आरण्यक), **Upaniṣad** (उपनिषद्), **Vedānta** (वेदान्त), **Pariśiṣṭa** (परिशिष्ट), **Upaveda** (उपवेद), **Fifth Veda** (*Pañcama Veda*, पञ्चम वेद), and **Jātaveda** (*Jātavedas*, जातवेदस्) belong to distinct structural levels:

1. **Epistemological Authority (*Śruti* vs. *Smṛti* / *Anu-Veda*):**
   * **Śruti** ("that which is heard"): Texts regarded in orthodox (*Āstika*) Hindu traditions as *Apauruṣeya* ("not of human agency," authorless, eternal, and revealed to ancient sages or *Ṛṣis* after intense meditation). *Śruti* comprises the four canonical Vedas (*Caturveda*) and their four internal textual layers (*Saṃhitā*, *Brāhmaṇa*, *Āraṇyaka*, and *Upaniṣad*).
   * **Smṛti** ("that which is remembered") or **Anu-Veda** (post-Vedic and ancillary literature): Auxiliary sciences, appendices, indices, applied technical disciplines, legal codes, epics, and popularized canonical scriptures composed by human authors. This sphere includes the six *Vedāṅgas* (and their *Sūtra* literature), *Pariśiṣṭas*, *Anukramaṇīs*, *Upavedas*, *Itihāsas*, *Purāṇas*, *Dharmaśāstras*, and "Fifth Veda" traditions.
2. **Pedagogical and Soteriological Progression (*Karma-Kāṇḍa* → *Upāsanā-Kāṇḍa* → *Jñāna-Kāṇḍa*):**
   * **Twofold Division (Ādi Śaṅkara and Classical Mīmāṃsā):** Divides the Vedic corpus into the **Karma-Kāṇḍa** (*Pūrva-kāṇḍa*, the sphere of ritual action comprising the *Saṃhitās* and *Brāhmaṇas*, and in some classifications the *Āraṇyakas*) and the **Jñāna-Kāṇḍa** (*Uttara-kāṇḍa*, the sphere of liberating knowledge comprising the *Upaniṣads* and often the *Āraṇyakas*, designated as *Vedānta*).
   * **Threefold Progression:** Traces the evolution from external liturgical hymns and sacrificial actions (*Karma-Kāṇḍa*: *Saṃhitā* and *Brāhmaṇa*), through esoteric forest meditations and internalized symbolic ritual (*Upāsanā-Kāṇḍa*: *Āraṇyaka*), to ultimate metaphysical realization of *Ātman* and *Brahman* (*Jñāna-Kāṇḍa*: *Upaniṣad* / *Vedānta*).
   * **Fourfold (and Fivefold) Internal Stratification:** While the standard classification (followed by traditional commentators and modern scholars such as Max Müller) divides each Veda into four layers (*Saṃhitā*, *Brāhmaṇa*, *Āraṇyaka*, *Upaniṣad*), some scholars and traditions add a fifth internal subdivision, **Upāsanā** (sections dedicated to meditative and devotional worship).
   * **Typological Fluidity:** These four internal divisions represent a literary typology rather than four uniformly separated physical books within every Vedic branch (*Śākhā*). In practice, the layers overlap and their boundaries are fluid: the *Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda Saṃhitās* embed *Brāhmaṇa* prose within the *Saṃhitā*; several *Brāhmaṇas* contain *Āraṇyakas* in their concluding sections; and the oldest *Upaniṣads* are physically embedded within *Brāhmaṇas*, *Āraṇyakas*, or even a *Saṃhitā* (such as the *Īśā Upaniṣad* forming Chapter 40 of the *Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā*).
3. **Exegetical and Authority-Claiming Traditions (*Vedānta* and *Pañcama Veda*):**
   * **Vedānta** functions both as a direct textual designation for the *Upaniṣads* (the physical and philosophical conclusion of the Vedas) and as the name of the later orthodox philosophical school (*Uttara Mīmāṃsā*) built upon the *Prasthānatrayī* (the *Upaniṣads*, *Bhagavad Gītā*, and *Brahma Sūtras*).
   * **Fifth Veda (*Pañcama Veda*)** is not a single canonical fifth *Śruti* scripture, but an honorific title of Vedic authority claimed by or accorded to post-Vedic Sanskrit and vernacular canons (such as *Itihāsa-Purāṇa*, the *Mahābhārata*, the *Rāmāyaṇa*, the *Nāṭyaśāstra*, *Āyurveda*, the *Rāmcaritmānas*, and Tamil devotional canons) that universalize Vedic *Dharma* and *Mokṣa* for all people.
4. **Ontological and Liturgical Axis (*Jātaveda*):**
   * **Jātaveda** (*Jātavedas*) is **not a text or a literary category**. It is a prominent Vedic Sanskrit epithet and manifestation of **Agni** (the god of fire)—specifically the terrestrial sacrificial altar fire (*Havyavāhana*) and "knower of all born beings / possessor of the Vedas"—around whom the liturgical invocations of the *Saṃhitās*, the ritual sacrifices of the *Brāhmaṇas*, and the meditative inquiries of the *Upaniṣads* revolve, and is later applied as an epithet to **Śiva** and **Viṣṇu**.

## 2. Functional Matrix of the Four Internal Layers and Cross-Veda Overview

### A. Functional Progression of the Four Internal Layers

Each of the four Vedas is transmitted through traditional schools or recensions (*Śākhās*). Within a given *Śākhā*, the corpus is categorized into four sequential literary layers that correspond to chronological phases of composition, liturgical/soteriological spheres (*Kāṇḍa*), the four stages of human life (*Āśrama*), and the two classical exegetical systems (*Mīmāṃsā*):

| Layer | Sanskrit Etymology | Approx. Chronology | Primary Content & Style | Liturgical / Spiritual Kāṇḍa | Traditional Āśrama (Life Stage) | Philosophical System (Darśana) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Saṃhitā** (संहिता) | *saṃ-dhā* ("put together, joined, collection") | c. 1500–900 BCE (*Ṛgveda* 2–9: c. 1500–1200 BCE; others: c. 1200–900 BCE) | Metrical hymns (*Ṛc*), sacrificial prose formulas (*Yajus*),litanies, benedictions, and liturgical melodies (*Sāman*) addressed to Vedic deities (*Devas*). | **Karma-Kāṇḍa** (*Pūrva* — Sphere of Ritual Action) | **Brahmacarya** (Celibate Student — Memorization of Mantras) | **Pūrva Mīmāṃsā** (Jaimini) |
| **2. ## 3. Exhaustive Breakdown and Inventory by Category

### I. The Vedas (*Caturveda* / *Śruti*)

* **Reference:** [Vedas — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas) | [Shakha — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakha)
* **Etymology and Definition:** Derived from the Sanskrit verbal root *vid-* ("to know"; Proto-Indo-European *\*weyd-*, cognate with Ancient Greek *οἶδα* [*oîda*] / *idea* and Latin *videō*), meaning "knowledge, wisdom." In orthodox (*Āstika*) Hindu philosophy (specifically *Mīmāṃsā* and *Vedānta*), the Vedas are held to be *Apauruṣeya* (unauthored by human or divine agency, eternal, and self-validating or *svataḥ-pramāṇa*) and *Śruti* ("that which is heard").
* **Trayī Vidyā vs. Caturveda:** Historically, the first three Vedas (*Ṛgveda*, *Yajurveda*, *Sāmaveda*) were grouped together as the **Trayī Vidyā** ("Triple Knowledge" or "Threefold Science") corresponding to the three main classes of sacrificial priests, before the *Atharvaveda* was integrated to form the fourfold canon (**Caturveda**) overseen by the *Brahman* priest. Purāṇic tradition (such as the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*) postulates a single original Veda that was divided into four by the sage Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana **Vedavyāsa** at the close of the *Dvāpara Yuga*.
* **Chronology and Oral Transmission (*Pāṭhas*):** Composed in Vedic Sanskrit between c. 1500 BCE and 500 BCE (spanning the Early to Late Vedic Period, from the Bronze Age to the Iron Age). The texts were transmitted orally with phonetic precision across millennia using eleven formal modes of recitation (**Ekādaśa-Pāṭhas**):
  * **3 Prakṛti-pāṭhas (Simple / Natural Recitations):**
    1. *Saṃhitā-pāṭha*: Continuous recitation of words bound together by euphonic *sandhi* rules.
    2. *Pada-pāṭha*: Word-by-word recitation dissolving *sandhi* and analyzing compound words (*samāsa*).
    3. *Krama-pāṭha*: Step-by-step progressive pairing of words ($1+2,\; 2+3,\; 3+4,\dots$).
  * **8 Vikṛti-pāṭhas (Complex Mnemonic Permutations to Prevent Textual Corruption):**
    1. *Jaṭā-pāṭha* ("mesh / braided locks"): Forward-reverse-forward pairing ($1\text{-}2\text{-}2\text{-}1\text{-}1\text{-}2$).
    2. *Mālā-pāṭha* ("garland"): Forward and backward linking across hemistichs ($krama$ forward and reverse).
    3. *Śikhā-pāṭha* ("crest / topknot"): Extends *Jaṭā-pāṭha* by adding the third word at the end of each cycle ($1\text{-}2\text{-}2\text{-}1\text{-}1\text{-}2\text{-}3$).
    4. *Rekhā-pāṭha* ("line"): Progressive linear step-expansions ($1\text{-}2,\; 2\text{-}3\text{-}4,\; 4\text{-}3\text{-}2,\dots$).
    5. *Dhvaja-pāṭha* ("flag"): Pairs the initial words of a hymn with the concluding words in inverse sequence ($1,\; N;\; 2,\; N-1;\dots$).
    6. *Daṇḍa-pāṭha* ("staff"): Cumulative forward and backward progressions ($1\text{-}2,\; 2\text{-}1,\; 1\text{-}2,\; 2\text{-}3,\; 3\text{-}2\text{-}1,\dots$).
    7. *Ratha-pāṭha* ("chariot"): Parallel wheel-like progressions across two or more pādas simultaneously.
    8. *Ghana-pāṭha* ("bell / dense"): The most intricate and prestigious permutation ($1\text{-}2\text{-}2\text{-}1\text{-}1\text{-}2\text{-}3\text{-}3\text{-}2\text{-}1\text{-}1\text{-}2\text{-}3$).

#### Exhaustive Inventory of the Four Vedas and Their Traditional Recensions (*Śākhās*)

According to Patañjali's *Mahābhāṣya* and the *Muktikā Upaniṣad* (1.1.14), the four Vedas were once transmitted across **1,180 Śākhās** (branches or theological schools, each originally possessing its own *Saṃhitā*, *Brāhmaṇa*, *Āraṇyaka*, *Upaniṣad*, and *Kalpasūtras*), of which only a small fraction survives:

1. **Ṛgveda (ऋग्वेद — Traditionally 21 Śākhās; 5 Principal Historical Branches in *Caraṇavyūha*):**
   * *Liturgical Role:* Assigned to the **Hotṛ** (invoker/reciter) priest; consists of *Ṛc* (metrical verses of praise).
   * *Recensions:*
     * **Śākala**: The **only fully extant** recension of the *Ṛgveda Saṃhitā*.
     * **Bāṣkala**: Partially preserved via the 36 supplementary *Khila-sūktas* (appended to the *Śākala Saṃhitā*) and the *Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇa* / *Āraṇyaka* / *Upaniṣad* tradition.
     * **Āśvalāyana**: Survives in its *Śrautasūtra*, *Gṛhyasūtra*, *Pariśiṣṭa*, and liturgical *Mantra-saṃhitā*.
     * **Śāṅkhāyana**: Survives in the *Śāṅkhāyana Brāhmaṇa*, *Āraṇyaka*, *Śrautasūtra*, and *Gṛhyasūtra* (with rare oral practitioners surviving in Banswara, Rajasthan).
     * **Māṇḍūkeya** (*Māṇḍūkāyana*): Listed in the *Caraṇavyūha* and cited in the *Aitareya Āraṇyaka*; lost as an independent *Saṃhitā*.
2. **Yajurveda (यजुर्वेद — Traditionally 109 Śākhās in *Muktikā* [or 100/101 in Patañjali]):**
   * *Liturgical Role:* Assigned to the **Adhvaryu** (executor/ritualist) priest; consists of *Yajus* (sacrificial prose formulas and verses). Divided into two major traditions:
   * **Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda (Traditionally 86 Śākhās):** Characterized by an unseparated mixture of *Mantra* verses and exegetical *Brāhmaṇa* prose directly inside the *Saṃhitā*:
     * **Taittirīya**: Fully extant (*Saṃhitā*, *Brāhmaṇa*, *Āraṇyaka*, *Upaniṣads*, and *Sūtras*); widespread across South India and Maharashtra.
     * **Maitrāyaṇī** (or *Kālāpa*): Extant (*Saṃhitā*, *Āraṇyaka*/*Upaniṣad*, *Mānava* and *Vārāha Sūtras*); survives among select Brahmin communities in Nashik and Gujarat.
     * **Caraka-Kaṭha** (*Kāṭhaka*): Extant in *Saṃhitā* manuscripts from Kashmir, fragments of its *Brāhmaṇa* and *Āraṇyaka*, the *Kaṭha Upaniṣad*, and the *Kāṭhaka Gṛhyasūtra*.
     * **Kapiṣṭhala-Kaṭha**: Extant only in fragmentary *Saṃhitā* manuscripts from Punjab/Kashmir.
   * **Śukla (White) Yajurveda (Traditionally 15 [or 16] Śākhās):** Attributed to Sage **Yājñavalkya**, who separated pure *Mantras* (*Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā*) from the explanatory *Brāhmaṇa* prose (*Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa*):
     * **Vājasaneyi Mādhyandina**: Fully extant; dominant across North India.
     * **Vājasaneyi Kāṇva**: Fully extant; preserved in Odisha, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, and Maharashtra.
3. **Sāmaveda (सामवेद — Traditionally 1,000 Śākhās):**
   * *Liturgical Role:* Assigned to the **Udgātṛ** (chanter/singer) priest; consists of *Sāman* (verses set to heptatonic/pentatonic musical melodies for Soma sacrifices).
   * *Recensions:*
     * **Kauthuma**: Fully extant; widest distribution across Gujarat, North India, and South India.
     * **Rāṇāyanīya**: Extant and closely allied to *Kauthuma*; preserved in Gokarna (Karnataka) and Maharashtra.
     * **Jaiminīya** (or *Talavakāra*): Extant with its own *Saṃhitā*, *Gānas*, *Brāhmaṇa*, and *Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa*; preserved by Nambudiri Brahmins in Kerala and Cōḻiya Brahmins in Tamil Nadu.
     * *Lost Śākhās cited in literature:* *Śāṭyāyana*, *Bhāllavī*, *Kālabavī*, *Raurukī*, *Naigeya* (survives as an index sub-school of *Kauthuma*).
4. **Atharvaveda (अथर्ववेद — Traditionally 50 Śākhās; 9 Listed in *Caraṇavyūha* and by Maurice Bloomfield):**
   * *Liturgical Role:* Assigned to the **Brahman** (supervising) priest, who silently oversees the entire *Yajña* and remedies ritual errors via expiatory mantras (*Prāyaścitta*); also serves as the manual of the royal chaplain (*Purohita*). Known historically as *Atharvāṅgirasaḥ*, *Bhṛgvāṅgirasaḥ*, *Brahmaveda*, or *Kṣatraveda*.
   * *Recensions:*
     * **Śaunaka** (*Śaunakīya*): Fully extant and standard printed recension.
     * **Paippalāda**: Extant via the birch-bark *Kashmirian Atharvaveda* manuscript and living oral and palm-leaf traditions discovered in Odisha (with reach into adjacent parts of West Bengal, Jharkhand, and Chhattisgarh).
     * *7 Lost Śākhās named in the Caraṇavyūha:* *Moda* (*Maudā*), *Staudā* (*Stauda*), *Jājala*, *Jalada*, *Brahmavada*, *Devadarśa* (*Devadarśin*), and *Cāraṇavaidya* (Note: the *Atharvaveda Pariśiṣṭas* warn against employing priests of the *Maudā* and *Jalada* schools without strict discipline).

#### Ancillary Vedic Disciplines: The Six *Vedāṅgas* and the *Anukramaṇīs*

1. **The Six Auxiliary Limbs of the Vedas (*Ṣaḍ-Vedāṅgas*):**
   Classified as *Smṛti* disciplines required to preserve, pronounce, interpret, and ritually apply the *Śruti* corpus (personified in Pāṇinīya Śikṣā 41–42 as the six bodily limbs of the *Veda-Puruṣa*):
   * **Śikṣā** ("Nose of the Veda" — Phonetics, Phonology, and Pitch Accents [*Udātta*, *Anudātta*, *Svarita*]): Includes the school-specific **Prātiśākhyas** (*Ṛgveda-Prātiśākhya* of Śaunaka, *Taittirīya-Prātiśākhya*, *Vājasaneyi-Prātiśākhya* of Kātyāyana, *Śaunakīyā Caturādhyāyikā* / *Atharvaveda-Prātiśākhya*, *Puṣpasūtra* and *Ṛktantra* of *Sāmaveda*) and general phonetic treatises (*Pāṇinīya Śikṣā*, *Nāradīya Śikṣā*, *Yājñavalkya Śikṣā*).
   * **Chandas** ("Feet of the Veda" — Prosody and Poetic Meter): Represented by Piṅgala's *Chandaḥśāstra*, analyzing the 7 primary Vedic meters (*Sapta-Chandas*): *Gāyatrī* (24 syllables), *Uṣṇih* (28), *Anuṣṭubh* (32), *Bṛhatī* (36), *Paṅkti* (40), *Triṣṭubh* (44), and *Jagatī* (48).
   * **Vyākaraṇa** ("Mouth of the Veda" — Grammar and Linguistic Analysis): Represented by the *Trimuni* ("Three Sages") of Sanskrit grammar: Pāṇini's *Aṣṭādhyāyī*, Kātyāyana's *Vārttikakāra*, and Patañjali's *Mahābhāṣya*.
   * **Nirukta** ("Ears of the Veda" — Etymology and Lexicography): Represented by Yāska's *Nirukta*, a systematic commentary on the *Nighaṇṭu* (lexicon of rare and archaic Vedic words).
   * **Jyotiṣa** ("Eyes of the Veda" — Observational Astronomy and Ritual Calendar/Timekeeping): Represented by Lagadha's *Vedāṅga Jyautiṣa* (preserved in an *Ārca* / *Ṛgvedic* recension of 36 verses and a *Yājuṣa* / *Yajurvedic* recension of 43 verses).
   * **Kalpa** ("Hands/Arms of the Veda" — Ritual Instruction Manuals in concise *Sūtra* style), subdivided into four genres (*Kalpasūtras*):
     * **Śrautasūtras** (Public, solemn multi-fire *Yajña* sacrifices): *Āśvalāyana*, *Śāṅkhāyana* (RV); *Lāṭyāyana*, *Drāhyāyaṇa*, *Jaiminīya* (SV); *Baudhāyana*, *Āpastamba*, *Hiraṇyakeśin* (*Satyāṣāḍha*), *Vaikhānasa*, *Bhāradvāja*, *Vādhūla*, *Mānava*, *Vārāha* (KYV); *Kātyāyana* (SYV); *Vaitāna* (AV).
     * **Gṛhyasūtras** (Domestic rites of passage / *Saṃskāras*): *Āśvalāyana*, *Śāṅkhāyana*, *Kauṣītaka* (RV); *Gobhila*, *Khādira*, *Jaiminīya* (SV); *Baudhāyana*, *Āpastamba*, *Hiraṇyakeśin*, *Bhāradvāja*, *Mānava*, *Kāṭhaka*, *Agniveśya* (KYV); *Pāraskara* (SYV); *Kauśika* (AV).
     * **Dharmasūtras** (Law, social ethics, civil and religious duty, which later expanded into the metrical **Dharmaśāstras** such as *Manusmṛti* and *Yājñavalkya Smṛti*): *Vasiṣṭha* (RV); *Gautama* (SV); *Baudhāyana*, *Āpastamba*, *Hiraṇyakeśin*, *Vaikhānasa*, *Viṣṇu* (KYV); *Śaṅkha-Likhita* (SYV).
     * **Śulbasūtras** (Sacrificial fire-altar geometry, containing early formulations of the Pythagorean theorem and accurate rational approximations of $\sqrt{2}$): *Baudhāyana*, *Āpastamba*, *Mānava*, *Kātyāyana*, *Maitrāyaṇīya*, *Vārāha*.

2. **Anukramaṇīs (Systematic Vedic Indices):**
   * **Reference:** [Anukramaṇī — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anukrama%E1%B9%87%C4%AB)
   * **Definition:** The *Anukramaṇīs* (also *Anukramaṇikās*) are canonical indices of Vedic hymns that record the opening word (*pratīka*), number of verses, traditional author/seer (*Ṛṣi* or female *Ṛṣikā*, of whom ~27–30 are named in the *Ṛgveda*, such as Ghoṣā, Lopāmudrā, Viśvavārā, Apālā, Vāc Āmbhṛṇī, and Gargī), deity (*Devatā*), and poetic meter (*Chandas*), without which *Śrauta* and *Smārta* rituals cannot be validly performed.
   * **Inventory by Veda:**
     * *Ṛgveda:* Six metrical indices attributed to **Śaunaka** (*Anuvākānukramaṇī* [extant], *Ārṣānukramaṇī*, *Chandonukramaṇī*, *Devatānukramaṇī*, *Padānukramaṇī*, and *Sūktānukramaṇī* [the last five surviving via quotations in Ṣaḍguruśiṣya]), plus Śaunaka's *Bṛhaddevatā* and *Ṛgvidhāna*. The preeminent index is **Kātyāyana's Sarvānukramaṇī** (c. 4th–2nd century BCE, composed in concise *Sūtra* style covering all 1,028 hymns, commented upon by Ṣaḍguruśiṣya in the 12th-century *Vedārthadīpikā*).
     * *Sāmaveda:* The *Ārṣeya Brāhmaṇa* (earliest *Anukramaṇī* of the *Sāmaveda*), *Jaiminīya Ārṣeya Brāhmaṇa*, and the two *Pariśiṣṭa* indices (*Ārṣa* and *Daivata*) of the *Naigeya* branch.
     * *Yajurveda:* Three principal *Anukramaṇīs* belonging to the *Ātreyī* school of the *Taittirīya* (in two parts: prose and *ślokas*), the *Cārāyaṇīya* school of the *Kāṭhaka* (attributed to Atri and Laugākṣi), and the *Mādhyandina* school of the *Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā* (attributed to Kātyāyana).
     * *Atharvaveda:* The *Bṛhat-Sarvānukramaṇī* (and the *Pañcapaṭalikā*).

### II. Saṃhitā (The Mantra Layer)

* **Reference:** [Samhita — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samhita)
* **Etymology and Definition:** Literally "put together, joined, union, collection" (from *sam* + *dhā*). In its primary Vedic sense, it denotes the oldest, foundational layer of each Veda, consisting of *Mantras* (hymns, prayers, litanies, sacrificial formulas, and benedictions) joined according to euphonic *sandhi* rules. In post-Vedic Sanskrit, the title *Saṃhitā* is also applied to systematic canonical compendia in *Āyurveda*, *Jyotiṣa*, *Purāṇa*, *Dharmaśāstra*, *Yoga*, and *Tantra/Āgama*.

### III. Brāhmaṇa (The Ritual Exegesis Layer)

* **Reference:** [Brahmana — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmana)
* **Etymology, Synonyms, and Chronology:** Derived from *brahman* ("sacred prayer, formula, or Vedic priest"). In *Brāhmaṇa* texts such as *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* 6.7.4.6, synonyms for *Brāhmaṇa* include *Vyākhyāna* ("explanation, elucidation, lecture"), *Pravacana* ("oral instruction, exposition"), and the citational marker *Vijñāyate* ("it is known [from a Brāhmaṇa]"). Composed between **c. 900 BCE and 700 BCE** in Middle to Late Vedic Sanskrit prose (with occasional metrical *gāthās*), they form the second layer of each Veda, instructing priests on the performance of Vedic sacrifices (*Yajña*) while also recording observational astronomy, altar geometry, myths, and early doctrines of karma and reincarnation.
* **Internal Division of Content (*Vidhi* and *Arthavāda*):**
  1. **Vidhi** (Injunctions / Directives): Commands prescribing how, when, and by whom a ritual action must be performed.
  2. **Arthavāda** (Explanatory / Eulogistic Glosses): Etymologies (*Nirukta*), myths (*Itihāsa*/*Purāṇa*), cosmic correspondences (*Bandhutā*), and praises of the rituals and *Devas* motivating the sacrifice.
* **Ten Traditional Explanatory Modes (*Daśavidha-Brāhmaṇārtha*):** According to Sāyaṇa and the *Mīmāṃsā* tradition, *Brāhmaṇas* employ ten modes of explanation: *Hetu* (reason), *Nirvacana* (etymology), *Nindā* (censure of improper practice), *Praśaṃsā* (praise of proper practice), *Saṃśaya* (doubt), *Vidhi* (injunction), *Parakṛti* (deed of another individual), *Purākalpa* (ancient legendary history), *Vyavadhāraṇakalpanā* (contextual determination of limits), and *Upamāna* (analogy).

#### Accounting for the 19 Surviving *Brāhmaṇas*

Standard Vedic scholarship notes that **19 Brāhmaṇas** survive into modern times (alongside several fragmentary texts). Depending on whether the embedded *Brāhmaṇa* prose layers of the *Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda* and the sub-*Brāhmaṇas* of the *Sāmaveda* are counted separately or within their parent compilations, the 19 texts are distributed in two ways:

* **Distribution Scheme A (Counting School-Specific Yajurveda Layers — 2 RV + 6 YV + 10 SV + 1 AV = 19):**
  * **Ṛgveda (2):** *Aitareya*, *Kauṣītaki* (*Śāṅkhāyana*).
  * **Yajurveda (6):** The 4 *Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda* school traditions (*Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa* + the embedded *Brāhmaṇa* prose of the *Maitrāyaṇī*, *Kāṭhaka*, and *Kapiṣṭhala-Kaṭha* schools) + the 2 recensions of the *Śukla Yajurveda* *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* (*Mādhyandina* and *Kāṇva*).
  * **Sāmaveda (10):** The 8 canonical *Brāhmaṇas* of the *Kauthuma/Rāṇāyanīya* school enumerated by Sāyaṇa (*Pañcaviṃśa*/*Tāṇḍya*, *Ṣaḍviṃśa* [including *Adbhuta*], *Sāmavidhāna*, *Ārṣeya*, *Devatādhyāya*/*Daivata*, *Chāndogya*/*Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa* [including *Mantra Brāhmaṇa*], *Saṃhitopaniṣad*, *Vaṃśa*) + 2 primary *Jaiminīya* texts (*Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa* and *Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa*, or counting *Jaiminīya Ārṣeya* when *JUB* is classed as an *Āraṇyaka*/*Upaniṣad*).
  * **Atharvaveda (1):** *Gopatha Brāhmaṇa*.
* **Distribution Scheme B (Counting Standalone Textual Titles — 2 RV + 5 YV + 11 SV + 1 AV = 19):**
  * **Ṛgveda (2):** *Aitareya*, *Kauṣītaki*.
  * **Yajurveda (5):** *Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa*, *Kāṭhaka Brāhmaṇa*, *Vādhūla Brāhmaṇa* (*Anvākhyāna*), *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa (Mādhyandina)*, *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa (Kāṇva)*.
  * **Sāmaveda (11):** The 8 *Kauthuma/Rāṇāyanīya Brāhmaṇas* + all 3 *Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇas* (*Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa*, *Jaiminīya Ārṣeya Brāhmaṇa*, *Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa*) (Note: when *Adbhuta Brāhmaṇa* and *Mantra Brāhmaṇa* are detached from *Ṣaḍviṃśa* and *Chāndogya*, the *Kauthuma* list alone yields 10 titles).
  * **Atharvaveda (1):** *Gopatha Brāhmaṇa*.

#### Exhaustive Inventory of All Extant *Brāhmaṇas*

1. **Ṛgveda Brāhmaṇas (2 Extant):**
   * **Aitareya Brāhmaṇa** (*AB*; rarely *Āśvalāyana Brāhmaṇa* — *Śākala Śākhā*): **8 Pañcikās** (Pentads) totaling **40 Adhyāyas** (Chapters) and **285 Khaṇḍas**. Attributed to Mahidāsa Aitareya; deals with Soma sacrifices (*Agniṣṭoma*, *Gavāmayana*, *Dvādaśāha*), the daily *Agnihotra*, and the royal consecration (*Rājasūya*, including the narrative of Śunaḥśepa in Pañcikā 7). Section 3.44 famously states that the Sun neither truly rises nor sets, but produces day below and night on the other side.
   * **Kauṣītaki Brāhmaṇa** (*KB*; also called **Śāṅkhāyana Brāhmaṇa** — *Bāṣkala / Śāṅkhāyana Śākhā*): **30 Adhyāyas** and **226 Khaṇḍas**. Adhyāyas 1–6 cover setting up the sacred fires (*Agnyādhāna*), *Agnihotra*, New and Full Moon sacrifices (*Darśapūrṇamāsa*), and seasonal sacrifices (*Cāturmāsyas*); Adhyāyas 7–30 cover Soma sacrifices.
2. **Sāmaveda Brāhmaṇas (11 Extant Titles across Two Recensions; 13 Counting Sub-Brāhmaṇas):**
   * *Kauthuma & Rāṇāyanīya Śākhās (The Traditional "Eight Brāhmaṇas" / Aṣṭa-Brāhmaṇa commented upon by Sāyaṇa):*
     1. **Tāṇḍya Mahābrāhmaṇa** (or **Pañcaviṃśa Brāhmaṇa** [*PB*] / *Prauḍha Brāhmaṇa*): **25 Prapāṭhakas** (Books). Describes the *Gavāmayana*, *Agniṣṭoma*, *Ukthya*, *Atirātra*, *Sattra* (multi-day sessions), and *Vrātyastoma* rites, and speculates on mountain-fed surface and subterranean rivers emptying into the sea.
     2. **Ṣaḍviṃśa Brāhmaṇa** (*ṢaḍvB*): Literally the "26th Brāhmaṇa" (supplement to the 25-book *Pañcaviṃśa*). Contains **5 Prapāṭhakas**; its 5th Prapāṭhaka is the **Adbhuta Brāhmaṇa** (often counted as a separate text dealing with omens, portents, earthquakes, and expiatory rites).
     3. **Sāmavidhāna Brāhmaṇa** (*SVB*): **3 Prapāṭhakas** in late *Sūtra*-like style; details magical, expiatory (*Prāyaścitta*), and domestic uses of *Sāman* chants, serving as a precursor to *Dharmaśāstra*.
     4. **Ārṣeya Brāhmaṇa** (*ĀrṣB*): **3 Prapāṭhakas**; the earliest *Anukramaṇī* index of the *Sāmaveda*, cataloging the *Ṛṣis* (seers) of the chants.
     5. **Devatādhyāya Brāhmaṇa** (or **Daivata Brāhmaṇa**): **3 Khaṇḍas** (4 in some manuscripts, having 26, 11, and 25 *Kaṇḍikās*); indexes the deities (*Devatās*) and meters (*Chandas*) of the *Sāmans*.
     6. **Chāndogya Brāhmaṇa** (*ChB*; also called **Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa**): **10 Prapāṭhakas** (Chapters). Prapāṭhakas 1–2 constitute the **Mantra Brāhmaṇa** (often counted separately; each Prapāṭhaka has 8 *Khaṇḍas* of domestic ritual mantras for marriage and birth ceremonies), while Prapāṭhakas 3–10 constitute the **Chāndogya Upaniṣad**.
     7. **Saṃhitopaniṣad Brāhmaṇa**: **1 Prapāṭhaka** divided into **5 Khaṇḍas**; discusses the esoteric effects of chanting *Sāmans* according to recitational combinations (*Saṃhitā*), melodies, and accents, plus rules for spiritual studentship.
     8. **Vaṃśa Brāhmaṇa**: **1 Chapter** (**3 Khaṇḍas**); records the genealogical succession (*Guru-śiṣya paramparā*) of 53 *Sāmaveda* teachers back to Kaśyapa, who received the teaching from the god Agni.
   * *Jaiminīya / Talavakāra Śākhā (3 Extant):*
     9. **Jaiminīya Brāhmaṇa** (*JB*): **3 Kāṇḍas** (360+ *Khaṇḍas*). Archaic and rich in narrative myths and cosmological dialogues (e.g., Bhṛgu Vāruṇi's vision of the afterlife).
     10. **Jaiminīya Ārṣeya Brāhmaṇa**: Index of *Sāman* seers belonging to the *Jaiminīya* school.
     11. **Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa** (*JUB*; also called **Talavakāra Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa**): **4 Books** (*Anuvākas*); functions simultaneously as an *Āraṇyaka* and *Upaniṣad*, embedding the **Kena Upaniṣad** in Book 4 (Chapters 18–21).
3. **Yajurveda Brāhmaṇas (5 Standalone / Fragmentary Texts + 4 Embedded *Saṃhitā* Prose Layers):**
   * *Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda:*
     12. **Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa** (*TB*): **3 Kāṇḍas** (*Aṣṭakas*) divided into **28 Prapāṭhakas** (commented upon by Bhaṭṭa Bhāskara Miśra and Sāyaṇa). Continues the *Taittirīya Saṃhitā* (which already embeds *Brāhmaṇa* prose); covers *Agnyādhāna*, *Gavāmayana*, *Vājapeya*, *Somayāga*, *Nakṣatreṣṭi*, *Rājasūya*, *Aśvamedha*, *Puruṣamedha*, and the *Kāṭhaka* fire-altar rites (precursor to the *Kaṭha Upaniṣad*, including the Nachiketa narrative in 3.11.8).
     13. **Kāṭhaka Brāhmaṇa** (Fragmentary + Embedded): Embedded within the *Kāṭhaka Saṃhitā* and preserved in standalone fragments (portions absorbed into *Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa* 3.10–12).
     14. **Vādhūla Brāhmaṇa** (*Anvākhyāna*): Preserved as the *Brāhmaṇa*-style exegetical layer attached to the *Vādhūla Śrautasūtra*.
     * *(Note: In the **Maitrāyaṇī** and **Kapiṣṭhala-Kaṭha** schools, the Brāhmaṇa prose is entirely interspersed within their respective Saṃhitās rather than surviving as separate books).*
   * *Śukla (White) Yajurveda:*
     15. **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa — Mādhyandina Recension** (*ŚBM*): **14 Kāṇḍas** (Books), **100 Adhyāyas** ("Brāhmaṇa of One Hundred Paths"), **68 Prapāṭhakas**, **438 Brāhmaṇas**, and **7,624 Kaṇḍikās** (filling 5 volumes of the *Sacred Books of the East* translated by Julius Eggeling). Kāṇḍas 1–5 expound Yājñavalkya's ritual system (Books 1–18 of VSM); Kāṇḍas 6–10 expound Śāṇḍilya's fire-altar (*Agnicayana*) theology; Kāṇḍas 11–13 cover supplementary rites (*Aśvamedha*, *Upanayana*, *Svādhyāya*); Kāṇḍa 14 is the *Bṛhad-Āraṇyaka*, whose final 6 chapters (14.4–9) form the **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad**. Contains the earliest Hindu flood myth (Manu and the *Matsya* / Fish in *ŚBM* 1.8.1).
     16. **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa — Kāṇva Recension** (*ŚBK*): **17 Kāṇḍas**, **104 Adhyāyas**, **435 Brāhmaṇas**, and **6,806 Kaṇḍikās** (Kāṇḍa 17 forms the Kāṇva *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad*).
4. **Atharvaveda Brāhmaṇa (1 Extant):**
   * **Gopatha Brāhmaṇa** (*GB* — shared by *Śaunaka* and *Paippalāda Śākhās*): **2 Bhāgas** (Books): *Pūrva-Gopatha* (5 *Prapāṭhakas*, 135 *Kaṇḍikās*) and *Uttara-Gopatha* (6 *Prapāṭhakas*, 123 *Kaṇḍikās*). The youngest of the canonical *Brāhmaṇas*; exalts the *Brahman* supervising priest and the *Atharvaveda*, discusses the cosmogonic origin of the syllable *Oṃ* (*Praṇava*) and *Gāyatrī*, and adapts ritual narratives from the *Aitareya*, *Kauṣītaki*, and *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇas*. Because the *Atharvaveda* lacks an independent *Āraṇyaka*, the *Gopatha Brāhmaṇa* is sometimes treated as fulfilling the function of its *Āraṇyaka*.

#### Lost and Fragmentary *Brāhmaṇas* (*Lupta-Brāhmaṇas*)

Known through citations in Pāṇini, Patañjali, Yāska, Ādi Śaṅkara, Sāyaṇa, and *Kalpa*/*Dharma* commentators (collected by Batakrishna Ghosh and Willem Caland):

* **Ṛgveda:** *Paiṅgya Brāhmaṇa* (*Paiṅgī*), *Bahvṛca Brāhmaṇa*, *Āśvalāyana Brāhmaṇa*, *Gālava Brāhmaṇa*.
* **Sāmaveda:** *Śāṭyāyana Brāhmaṇa* (heavily cited by Sāyaṇa), *Bhāllavī Brāhmaṇa*, *Kālabavī Brāhmaṇa*, *Raurukī Brāhmaṇa*, *Tumburu Brāhmaṇa*.
* **Yajurveda:** *Chāgaleya Brāhmaṇa*, *Hāridravika Brāhmaṇa*, *Ahvaraka Brāhmaṇa*, *Kāṅkati Brāhmaṇa*, *Māṣaśarāvī Brāhmaṇa*, *Śvetāśvatara Brāhmaṇa*, *Jābāla Brāhmaṇa*, *Khāṇḍikīya Brāhmaṇa*, *Aukheya Brāhmaṇa*.

### IV. Āraṇyaka (The "Forest Books" / Esoteric Ritual Layer)

* **Reference:** [Aranyaka — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aranyaka)
* **Etymology, Chronology, and Definition:** Derived from *araṇya* ("forest, wilderness"); composed around **c. 700 BCE**. According to the *Taittirīya Āraṇyaka* and Sāyaṇa (*araṇye bhavam araṇyam; araṇye'dhyayanād etad āraṇyakam itīryate*), these treatises were studied outside the village in the seclusion of the forest because they dealt with esoteric, mystical, and ritually dangerous ceremonies (such as the *Mahāvrata* solstice rite and the *Pravargya* glowing-cauldron Soma rite) whose potencies were deemed unsafe for domestic settlements.
* **Heterogeneity and Structural Role:** The *Āraṇyakas* are not stylistically homogeneous: some chapters read like a *Saṃhitā* (mantra collections), some like a *Brāhmaṇa* (ritual prose), and some like a *Sūtra* (concise aphoristic rules). They form the **Upāsanā-Kāṇḍa** (internalized, symbolic, and meditative sacrifice), bridging the *Brāhmaṇas* and the *Upaniṣads*, with most ancient *Upaniṣads* directly embedded inside them.

### V. Upaniṣads (The Philosophical Culmination Layer)

* **Reference:** [Upanishads — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanishads)
* **Etymology and Definition:** *Upa* ("near") + *ni* ("down") + *ṣad* ("to sit") — "sitting down near a spiritual teacher to receive esoteric instruction." According to Ādi Śaṅkara, the root *sad-* signifies "to destroy, loosen, or attain," denoting *Brahmavidyā* (knowledge of *Brahman*) that destroys innate ignorance (*Avidyā*) and loosens the bonds of transmigration (*Saṃsāra*).
* **Core Doctrines and Key Thinkers:** The *Upaniṣads* shift the focus of Vedic religion from external sacrificial ritualism to interiorized meditation (*Upāsanā* / *Dhyāna*), ontology, and consciousness. They investigate **Brahman** (Ultimate Reality, both *Nirguṇa* [without attributes] and *Saguṇa* [with attributes]) and its identity or relationship with **Ātman** (the innermost Self/soul), alongside *Karma*, transmigration (*Saṃsāra*), *Māyā*, the sacred syllable *Oṃ* (*Praṇava*), the four states of consciousness (*Jāgrat* [waking], *Svapna* [dreaming], *Suṣupti* [deep sleep], and *Turīya* [the fourth, pure consciousness]), the five sheaths (*Pañcakośa*), and liberation (*Mokṣa* — both *Jīvanmukti* [liberation in life] and *Videhamukti* [bodiless liberation]). Prominent sages and interlocutors credited in the early *Upaniṣads* include Yājñavalkya, Uddālaka Āruṇi, Śvetaketu, Śāṇḍilya, Mahidāsa Aitareya, Bālāki, Pippalāda, Sanatkumāra, Naciketas, King Janaka, King Pravāhaṇa Jaivali, King Aśvapati Kaikeya, Raikva, and women philosophers such as **Maitreyī** and **Gārgī Vācaknavī** (while the *Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad* explicitly credits the sage Śvetāśvatara as its author).

#### 1. The Four Canonical *Mahāvākyas* ("Great Sayings")

In Advaita Vedānta (attributed to Ādi Śaṅkara), one supreme aphorism from each of the four Vedas is selected to express the non-dual identity of *Ātman* and *Brahman*:

1. **Prajñānaṃ Brahma** ("Consciousness is Brahman") — *Aitareya Upaniṣad* 3.3 (*Ṛgveda*)
2. **Ahaṃ Brahmāsmi** ("I am Brahman") — *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 1.4.10 (*Yajurveda*)
3. **Tat Tvam Asi** ("Thou Art That" / "That Thou Art") — *Chāndogya Upaniṣad* 6.8.7 (*Sāmaveda*)
4. **Ayam Ātmā Brahma** ("This Self [Ātman] is Brahman") — *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* 1.2 (*Atharvaveda*)

#### 2. The 13 Principal *Upaniṣads* (*Mukhya Upaniṣads* — c. 800 BCE – 100 BCE)

In the *Muktikā Upaniṣad* (1.1.26–29), Rāma declares: *"The Māṇḍūkya alone is sufficient for liberation; if knowledge is not attained from it, study the **Ten Upaniṣads** [Daśopaniṣads]; if certainty is still not attained, study the **32 Upaniṣads** and stop; if desiring Videhamukti (liberation without the body), study all **108 Upaniṣads**."*
Modern scholarship and Vedāntic tradition group **13 Upaniṣads** as the **Principal (*Mukhya*) Upaniṣads**—comprising the **10 Daśopaniṣads** commented upon by Ādi Śaṅkara (Nos. 1–10 below) plus **3 ancient Vedic Upaniṣads** (Nos. 11–13 below: *Śvetāśvatara*, *Kauṣītaki*, and *Maitrāyaṇī* / *Maitrī*) cited by Śaṅkara and Rāmānuja:

1. **Īśā (*Īśāvāsya*) Upaniṣad** (*Śukla Yajurveda* — *VSM/VSK* Chapter 40; the only Upaniṣad embedded directly in a *Saṃhitā*; *Muktikā* #1).
2. **Kena (*Talavakāra*) Upaniṣad** (*Sāmaveda* — *Jaiminīya Upaniṣad Brāhmaṇa* 4.18–21; *Muktikā* #2).
3. **Kaṭha (*Kāṭhaka* / *Kaṭhavallī*) Upaniṣad** (*Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda* — *Caraka-Kaṭha Śākhā*; dialogue of Nachiketa and Yama; *Muktikā* #3).
4. **Praśna Upaniṣad** (*Atharvaveda* — *Paippalāda Śākhā*; six philosophical questions posed to Sage Pippalāda; *Muktikā* #4).
5. **Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad** (*Atharvaveda* — *Śaunaka Śākhā*; source of India's national motto *Satyameva Jayate* and the distinction between *Parā Vidyā* [higher knowledge of the Imperishable] and *Aparā Vidyā* [lower knowledge of the four Vedas and six *Vedāṅgas*, 1.1.4–5]; *Muktikā* #5).
6. **Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad** (*Atharvaveda* — *Śaunaka Śākhā*; 12 prose verses analyzing *Oṃ* and the four states of consciousness; *Muktikā* #6).
7. **Taittirīya Upaniṣad** (*Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda* — *Taittirīya Āraṇyaka* Books 7–9; source of the *Pañcakośa* [five sheaths] doctrine; *Muktikā* #7).
8. **Aitareya Upaniṣad** (*Ṛgveda* — *Aitareya Āraṇyaka* Book 2, Chapters 4–6; *Muktikā* #8).
9. **Chāndogya Upaniṣad** (*Sāmaveda* — *Chāndogya Brāhmaṇa* Books 3–10; expounds *Śāṇḍilya-vidyā*, *Dahara-vidyā*, and Uddālaka's teaching to Śvetaketu; *Muktikā* #9).
10. **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad** (*Śukla Yajurveda* — *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* 14.4–9 [*Mādhyandina*] / Kāṇḍa 17 [*Kāṇva*]; oldest and longest Upaniṣad, featuring Yājñavalkya's dialogues with Maitreyī, Gārgī, and King Janaka; *Muktikā* #10).
11. **Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad** (*Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda* — *Śvetāśvatara Śākhā*; foundational for theistic Vedānta, *Śaivism*, *Sāṃkhya-Yoga*, and *Bhakti*; *Muktikā* #14).
12. **Maitrāyaṇīya (*Maitrī*) Upaniṣad** (Historically *Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda* — *Maitrāyaṇī Śākhā*, though assigned to *Sāmaveda* in the *Muktikā* Veda-list; bridges Vedic ritual, *Sāṃkhya-Yoga*, and *Vedānta*; *Muktikā* #24).
13. **Kauṣītaki (*Kauṣītaki-Brāhmaṇa*) Upaniṣad** (*Ṛgveda* — *Kauṣītaki Āraṇyaka* Chapters 3–6; *Muktikā* #25).

* **Chronological Strata of the 13 Principal Upaniṣads (per Patrick Olivelle, Paul Deussen, R. D. Ranade, and S. Radhakrishnan):**
  1. *Ancient Pre-Buddhist Prose (c. 800–500 BCE):* *Bṛhadāraṇyaka*, *Chāndogya*, *Taittirīya*, *Aitareya*, *Kauṣītaki*.
  2. *Early Metrical / Transitional (c. 500–200 BCE):* *Kena* (prose and verse), *Kaṭha*, *Īśā*, *Śvetāśvatara*, *Muṇḍaka*.
  3. *Later Prose (c. 200 BCE – 100 CE):* *Praśna*, *Maitrāyaṇī* (*Maitrī*), *Māṇḍūkya*.

#### 3. Complete Canonical Catalog of All 108 *Upaniṣads* of the *Muktikā* Canon

In the *Muktikā Upaniṣad* (predating 1656 CE; commented upon in full in the 18th century by Rāmacandrendra Sarasvatī, known as **Upaniṣad Brahmayogin**), the 108 Upaniṣads are enumerated in two ways:

* **By Serial Order (#1–108):** In Adhyāya 1, Khaṇḍa 1, Verses 30–39.
* **By Veda and Śānti-Mantra:** In Adhyāya 1, Khaṇḍa 2, Verses 1–5 (**Ṛgveda: 10**, **Śukla Yajurveda: 19**, **Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda: 32**, **Sāmaveda: 16**, **Atharvaveda: 31**; Total = **108**).
* **By Seven Thematic / Sectarian Categories:** Depending on whether the 3 additional Principal Upaniṣads (#14 *Śvetāśvatara*, #24 *Maitrāyaṇī*, #25 *Kauṣītaki*) are counted under *Mukhya* (making 13 *Mukhya* and 21 *Sāmānya Vedānta*) or under the *Adyar Library* thematic volumes (which separate the 10 *Daśopaniṣads* and publish 24 in the *Sāmānya Vedānta* volume, 17 in the *Sannyāsa* volume, 20 in the *Yoga* volume, 14 in the *Vaiṣṇava* volume, 14 in the *Śaiva* volume, and 8 in the *Śākta* volume [or 9 with *Annapūrṇā*]), the thematic counts across scholarly and traditional editions are:
  * **Mukhya (Principal):** **13** (or **10** *Daśopaniṣads*)
  * **Sāmānya Vedānta (General Metaphysical):** **21** (or **24** when including #14 *Śvetāśvatara*, #24 *Maitrāyaṇī*, #25 *Kauṣītaki*)
  * **Sannyāsa (Monastic / Renunciation):** **18** (or **17** in the Adyar *Sannyāsa* volume; **19–20** when including #98 *Varāha*, #104 *Jābāli*, or #24 *Maitrāyaṇī*)
  * **Yoga:** **20**
  * **Vaiṣṇava:** **14**
  * **Śaiva:** **14** (or **12–13** when #83 *Kaṭharudra* or #104 *Jābāli* are counted under *Sannyāsa*)
  * **Śākta:** **8** (or **9** when #70 *Annapūrṇā* is counted under *Śākta* rather than *Sāmānya*)

#### 4. Non-*Muktikā* and Regional *Upaniṣad* Anthologies (200+ Known Texts)

Beyond the Southern Indian **108-text *Muktikā* canon**, over 200 distinct texts bearing the title *Upaniṣad* survive across regional recensions:

* **Northern Indian 52-Text Collections (Colebrooke and Nārāyaṇa):** Henry Thomas Colebrooke's 1805 catalog and the 17th-century commentator Nārāyaṇa Bhaṭṭa's *Dīpikā* compilation both enumerate **52 Upaniṣads** (primarily Atharvan, counting the constituent parts of the *Nṛsiṃhatāpanīya*, *Rāmatāpanīya*, and *Gopālatāpanīya* separately).
* **Dārā Shukoh's 50-Text *Sirr-i-Akbar* (*Oupanekhat*, 1656 CE):** The Mughal prince Dārā Shukoh assembled pandits from Varanasi to translate **50 Upaniṣads** into Persian under the title *Sirr-i-Akbar* ("The Greatest Secret"), later translated into Latin by Abraham Hyacinthe Anquetil-Duperron in 1801–1802 as *Oupanek'hat* (which introduced Upaniṣadic thought to Arthur Schopenhauer and Western philosophy). This anthology uniquely preserves **4 ancient *Saṃhitā Upaniṣads*** lost or only recently rediscovered in Sanskrit: *Bāṣkalamantra Upaniṣad*, *Chāgaleya Upaniṣad*, *Ārṣeya Upaniṣad*, and *Śaunaka Upaniṣad* (alongside *Prāṇava*, *Śivasaṅkalpa*, and *Tad-eva*).
* **Later Tantric, Śākta, and Syncretic Upaniṣads:** Include the *Kaula Upaniṣad*, *Guhyakālī Upaniṣad*, *Kālikā Upaniṣad*, *Tārā Upaniṣad*, *Śrīvidyātāraka Upaniṣad*, *Caturvedaśikhā Upaniṣad*, and the Mughal-era syncretic *Allopaniṣad*.

### VI. Vedānta (The Exegetical Philosophical School & Textual Triad)

* **Reference:** [Vedanta — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedanta)
* **Etymology and Dual Meaning:** *Veda* ("knowledge") + *anta* ("end, conclusion, culmination, goal, or essence"):
  1. **Textual Definition:** Originally a direct synonym for the **Upaniṣads** themselves (first attested in *Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad* 6.22 and *Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad* 3.2.6), because the *Upaniṣads* stand at both the literary end and the philosophical pinnacle of the Vedic corpus.
  2. **Philosophical Definition (*Uttara Mīmāṃsā*):** One of the six orthodox (*Āstika*) *Darśanas* of Hindu philosophy. It is Paired with and contrasted against **Pūrva Mīmāṃsā** (which investigates the earlier ritualistic *Karma-Kāṇḍa*, opening with Jaimini's *Athāto Dharma-jijñāsā* ["Now, therefore, the inquiry into Dharma"]), whereas **Vedānta** (*Uttara Mīmāṃsā*, the "Latter/Higher Inquiry") investigates the *Jñāna-Kāṇḍa*, opening with Bādarāyaṇa's *Athāto Brahma-jijñāsā* ("Now, therefore, the inquiry into Brahman" — *Brahma Sūtra* 1.1.1).

#### 1. The Triple Canonical Foundation (*Prasthānatrayī*)

Every classical school (*Sampradāya*) of Vedānta establishes its systematic philosophy by interpreting three foundational authorities (**Prasthānatrayī**):

1. **Śruti-prasthāna (Revealed Foundation):** The Principal **Upaniṣads** (the 10–13 *Mukhya Upaniṣads*).
2. **Smṛti-prasthāna (Remembered Foundation):** The **Bhagavad Gītā** (700 verses from the *Bhīṣma Parva* [Chapters 23–40] of the *Mahābhārata*).
3. **Nyāya-prasthāna (Logical / Exegetical Foundation):** The **Brahma Sūtras** (also titled *Vedānta Sūtras*, *Śārīraka Sūtras*, *Uttara Mīmāṃsā Sūtras*, or *Bhikṣu Sūtras*), systematized by **Bādarāyaṇa** (c. 450 BCE – 200 CE). Structured into **4 Adhyāyas** (Chapters), each subdivided into **4 Pādas** (Quarters), totaling **191 Adhikaraṇas** (Topical Sections) and **555 Sūtras** (aphorisms):
   * *Adhyāya 1 — Samanvaya ("Harmony"):* Harmonizes seemingly diverse Upaniṣadic passages to demonstrate that *Brahman* is the sole origin, substrate, and goal of all existence.
   * *Adhyāya 2 — Avirodha ("Non-Contradiction"):* Refutes rival philosophical objections (*Sāṃkhya*, *Vaiśeṣika*, Buddhist schools, Jainism, *Pāśupata*, *Bhāgavata*).
   * *Adhyāya 3 — Sādhana ("Spiritual Means"):* Examines meditative disciplines (*Vidyās*), transmigration, and epistemological pathways to realization.
   * *Adhyāya 4 — Phala ("Fruit / Goal"):* Expounds the state of liberation (*Mokṣa* — both *Jīvanmukti* and *Videhamukti*).

### VII. Pariśiṣṭa (The Ancillary Supplements & Appendices)

* **Reference:** [Pariśiṣṭa — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pari%C5%9Bi%E1%B9%A3%E1%B9%ADa)
* **Etymology, Character, and Chronology:** Derived from *pari-śiṣ* ("that which is left over, remainder, supplement, appendix"). The *Pariśiṣṭas* constitute a specialized class of late-Vedic and post-Vedic ancillary treatises (c. 400 BCE – early centuries CE), described by Max Müller as "the very last outskirts of Vedic literature," which nevertheless remain genuinely Vedic in character. Written in a *Sūtra*-like style that combines verse and prose (less concise and simpler than the older *Kalpasūtras*), they expound "what remains to be told"—supplying details on rituals, domestic ceremonies, hymn classifications, omens, astrology, phonetics, canonical bibliographies, and scholarly opinions omitted from the preceding *Saṃhitās*, *Brāhmaṇas*, *Āraṇyakas*, and *Kalpasūtras*. They exist for all four Vedas, with the *Atharvaveda Pariśiṣṭas* forming by far the largest collection.

#### Exhaustive Inventory of All Extant *Pariśiṣṭas* Across the Four Vedas

1. **Ṛgveda Pariśiṣṭas:**
   * **Āśvalāyana Gṛhya Pariśiṣṭa**: A prose and metrical appendix in **3 Adhyāyas** attached to the *Āśvalāyana Gṛhyasūtra*; elaborates on domestic ceremonies, *Sandhyā*, *Grahayajña* (planetary propitiation), image installation (*Pratiṣṭhā*), and worship of late-Vedic/Purāṇic deities (*Sadāśiva*, *Durgā*, *Gajānana*).
   * **Bahvṛca Gṛhya Pariśiṣṭa**: Supplementary domestic ritual manual of the Ṛgvedic (*Bahvṛca*) tradition.
   * **Śāṅkhāyana Gṛhya Pariśiṣṭa**: Comprises Books 5 and 6 appended to the *Śāṅkhāyana Gṛhyasūtra*.
   * *(Note: In a broader sense, the 36 **Khila-sūktas** of the Bāṣkala recension—including the **Śrī Sūktam**—function as the mantra-supplements to the Ṛgveda Saṃhitā).*

2. **Sāmaveda Pariśiṣṭas:**
   * **Gobhila Gṛhya Pariśiṣṭa** (*Gobhilaputra's Gṛhyasaṃgraha*): A concise metrical treatise in **2 Prapāṭhakas** (**113** and **95** verses respectively) covering domestic rituals, sacred fire classification, and *Śrāddha* rites.
   * **Chāndogya Pariśiṣṭa** (also titled **Karmapradīpa** or *Kātyāyana Smṛti*): A metrical supplement in **3 Prapāṭhakas** attributed variously to Kātyāyana or Gobhila, expanding on domestic ceremonies and legal/ritual duties not fully covered in the *Gobhila Gṛhyasūtra*.
   * **Ārṣa and Daivata Indices**: Two short *Pariśiṣṭas* of the *Naigeya* sub-school of the *Kauthuma Śākhā*, cataloging the seers (*Ṛṣis*) and deities (*Devatās*) of *Ṛgvedic* verses adapted into the *Sāmaveda*.

3. **Yajurveda Pariśiṣṭas:**
   * **Śukla (White) Yajurveda — The 18 *Kātiya* (*Kātyāyana*) *Pariśiṣṭas*** (attributed to **Kātyāyana** and enumerated in the 5th *Pariśiṣṭa*, the *Caraṇavyūha*; across manuscript catalogs and Albrecht Weber's editions, minor title variants occur at #8, #14, and #18 as noted below):
     1. *Yūpalakṣaṇa* (Specifications of the sacrificial post)
     2. *Chāgalakṣaṇa* (Characteristics of the sacrificial goat)
     3. *Pratijñā Pariśiṣṭa* (Ritual vows and accents)
     4. *Anuvākasaṃkhyā* (Numerical index of sections in the *Vājasaneyi Saṃhitā*)
     5. *Caraṇavyūha* (Canonical treatise cataloging the *Śākhās*, divisions, and *Upavedas* of all four Vedas)
     6. *Śrāddhakalpa* (or *Śrāddhakalpasūtra* / *Navakaṇḍikā* — Ancestral *Śrāddha* funeral rites)
     7. *Śulbasūtra* (or *Śulba Pariśiṣṭa* / *Kātyāyana Śulbasūtra* — Sacrificial altar geometry)
     8. *Ṛgyajūṃṣi* (Concordance of *Ṛgvedic* and *Yajurvedic* verses)
     9. *Pārṣada* (*Vājasaneyi Prātiśākhya* phonetic rules)
     10. *Iṣṭakāpūraṇa* (Brick-laying rules for fire altars)
     11. *Pravarādhyāya* (Lineages of Vedic *Gotras* and *Pravaras*)
     12. *Uñchaśāstra* (Gleaning rules for ascetics and householders)
     13. *Nigama Pariśiṣṭa* (Vedic glossary containing 469 lexical items across 31 sections)
     14. *Kṛtākṛtāvekṣaṇa* (or *Yajñapārśva* / *Pravarādhyāya II* — Review of completed and omitted ritual acts)
     15. *Hautrika* (*Kātiya Hautra* — Duties of the *Hotṛ* priest)
     16. *Kūrmalakṣaṇa* (Placement of the tortoise under the *Agnicayana* altar)
     17. *Mūlyādhyāya* (or *Mūlyādhyāyapariśiṣṭa* — Rules on sacrificial fees / *Dakṣiṇā* substitutes)
     18. *Kuṇḍalakṣaṇa* (or *Unādipāṭha* / *Mahāvrata* supplement — Specifications of sacrificial fire-pits or *Uṇādi* suffixes)
   * **Kṛṣṇa (Black) Yajurveda — 3 Śrauta Pariśiṣṭas and Related Gṛhya Pariśiṣṭas:**
     1. *Āpastamba Hautra Pariśiṣṭa*: Preserved both independently and as the second *Praśna* of the *Satyāṣāḍha (Hiraṇyakeśin) Śrautasūtra*, specifying the duties of the *Hotṛ* priest.
     2. *Vārāha Śrautasūtra Pariśiṣṭa*: Supplementary *Śrauta* manual belonging to the *Maitrāyaṇī* school.
     3. *Kātyāyana Śrautasūtra Pariśiṣṭa*: A third *Śrauta Pariśiṣṭa* transmitted alongside the *Vārāha*/*Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda* ritual manuals (or counting the *Satyāṣāḍha Śrautasūtra* second *Praśna* separately).
     * *Additional Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda Gṛhya Pariśiṣṭas:* Include the *Kāṭhaka Gṛhya Pariśiṣṭa* and the *Baudhāyana Gṛhya Pariśiṣṭasūtra* (*Śeṣasūtra*, detailing late domestic and temple consecration rites).

4. **Atharvaveda Pariśiṣṭas (72 Distinctly Named *Pariśiṣṭas* / 79 Total Works):**
   Critically edited by George Melville Bolling and Julius von Negelein (*The Pariśiṣṭas of the Atharvaveda*, Leipzig, 1909–1910). Because seven entries contain *Pūrva* and *Uttara* or multiple sub-treatises, the **72 named Pariśiṣṭas** comprise **79 distinct works** covering royal consecration rites (*Rājakarmāṇi*), military lustration, judicial ordeals, astrology (*Nakṣatra* and *Graha*), omens/portents (*Adbhuta*), folklore, phonetics, and early *Śaiva*/*Śākta*/*Tantric* ritual:
   * **1–19 (Royal Ceremonies & Great Charitable Gifts / *Mahādānas*):** (1) *Nakṣatrakalpa*, (2) *Rāṣṭrasaṃvarga*, (3) *Rājaprathamābhiṣeka* (Royal Coronation), (4) *Purohitakarmāṇi* (Daily duties of the Royal Chaplain), (5) *Puṣyābhiṣeka*, (6) *Piṣṭarātryāḥ Kalpaḥ*, (7) *Ārātrika*, (8) *Ghṛtāvekṣaṇa*, (9) *Tiladhenuvidhi*, (10) *Bhūmidāna*, (11) *Tulāpuruṣavidhi*, (12) *Ādityamaṇḍaka*, (13) *Hiraṇyagarbhavidhi*, (14) *Hastyṛṣabhadānavidhi*, (15) *Aśvarathadānavidhi*, (16) *Gosahasravidhi*, (17–18) *Rājakarmasāṃvatsarīya*, (19) *Brahmayāga*.
   * **20–36 (Liturgical, Magical & Witchcraft Manuals):** (20) *Skandayāga* (*Dhūrtakalpa* — Worship of Skanda as patron of rogues/thieves), (21–29) *Sambhāra*, *Araṇi*, *Yajñapātra*, *Vedi*, *Kuṇḍa*, *Samidh*, *Sruc*, *Hasta*, and *Mudrā Lakṣaṇas* (early Tantric *Mudrās* and *Maṇḍalas*), (30–31) *Laghu-* & *Bṛhal-lakṣahoma* and *Koṭihoma*, (32) *Gaṇamālā* (thematic groupings of Atharvan hymns), (33) *Ghṛtakambala*, (34) *Anulomakalpa*, (35) *Āsurīkalpa* (witchcraft employing the black mustard plant), (36) *Ucchuṣmakalpa* (worship of Rudra-Ucchuṣma).
   * **37–46 (Expiations, Consecrations & Sectarian Vows):** (37) *Abhicārakalpa*, (38) *Dhvajāharaṇa*, (39) *Taḍāgādividhi* (consecration of tanks and wells), (40) *Pāśupatavrata* (ascetic *Śaiva* vow), (41–44) *Sandhyā*, *Snāna*, *Tarpaṇa*, and *Śrāddha Vidhis*, (45–46) *Agnihotra* & *Uttamapaṭala*.
   * **47–49 (Linguistic, Phonetic & Canonical Indexes):** (47) *Varṇapaṭala* (phonetics), (48) *Kautsavya Niruktanighaṇṭu* (Atharvan lexicon), (49) *Caraṇavyūha* of the *Atharvaveda* (enumerating the 9 Atharvan *Śākhās*, 72 *Pariśiṣṭas*, and *Upaniṣads*).
   * **50–72 (Astrology, Meteorology & Portents / *Adbhutaśānti*):** (50–56) *Candraprātipadika*, *Grahasaṃgraha*, *Rāhucāra*, *Ketucāra*, *Kūrmavibhāga* (astrological geography), (57) *Maṇḍalāni*, (58–63) *Digdāha*, *Ulkā*, *Vidyut*, *Nirghāta*, *Bhūmikampa* (Earthquakes), and *Pariveṣa* (Halos) *Lakṣaṇas*, (64) *Nakṣatragrahaśānti*, (65) *Sadyovṛṣṭilakṣaṇa* (rain forecasting), (66) *Gośānti*, (67) *Adbhutaśānti*, (68) *Svapnādhyāya* (dream interpretation), (69) *Atharvahṛdaya*, (70) *Bhārgavīyāṇi*, (71) *Auśanasādbhūtāni*, (72) *Mahābhūtāni*.

### VIII. Upaveda (The Four Applied Sciences / Sub-Vedas)

* **Reference:** [Vedas #Upaveda — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas#Upaveda)
* **Etymology and Structural Status:** *Upa* ("subsidiary, secondary, applied") + *Veda* ("knowledge"). An *Upaveda* is not a fifth internal layer inside the four Vedas; rather, it designates one of four secular or technical disciplines of applied science traditionally affiliated with the four canonical Vedas. While all traditional lists agree on linking **Dhanurveda** to the *Yajurveda* and **Gāndharvaveda** to the *Sāmaveda*, classical authorities (such as the Śukla Yajurvedic and Atharvan recensions of the *Caraṇavyūha*, the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*, the *Suśruta Saṃhitā*, the *Caraka Saṃhitā*, the *Bhāvaprakāśa*, and Madhusūdana Sarasvatī's *Prasthānabheda*) preserve two main variants for the *Ṛgveda* and *Atharvaveda* affiliations and alternate between **Sthāpatyaveda** (*Śilpaśāstra* / Architecture) and **Arthaśāstra** (Statecraft / Political Economy) for the fourth *Upaveda*:

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Āyurveda** | Medicine, Anatomy, Pharmacology, Surgery & Life Science | **Ṛgveda** | **Atharvaveda** (per *Suśruta Saṃhitā* 1.1.6 & *Caraka Saṃhitā* 30.21, due to the AV's extensive *Bhaiṣajya* healing hymns) | **Bṛhat-Trayī** (*Great Triad*): *Caraka Saṃhitā*, *Suśruta Saṃhitā*, *Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya* (Vāgbhaṭa). **Laghu-Trayī** (*Lesser Triad*): *Mādhava Nidāna*, *Śārṅgadhara Saṃhitā*, *Bhāvaprakāśa*. |
| **2. Dhanurveda** | Archery, Martial Arts, Weaponry, Chariotry & Warfare | **Yajurveda** | **Yajurveda** | *Dhanurveda Saṃhitā* of Viśvāmitra, Vasiṣṭha's *Dhanurveda*, *Śiva Dhanurveda*, *Kodaṇḍamaṇḍana*, *Nītiprakāśikā* (Vaiśampāyana), and martial chapters of *Agni Purāṇa* (Ch. 249–252). |

### IX. Fifth Veda (*Pañcama Veda* — Universalized Post-Vedic Canons)

* **Reference:** [Fifth Veda — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Veda)
* **Concept and Theological Justification:** Canonical *Śruti* is strictly limited to the four Vedas (*Caturveda*). However, because access to formal Sanskrit Vedic recitation was historically restricted by ritual qualification (*Adhikāra* / *Upanayana* initiation of the three *Dvija* ["twice-born"] *Varṇas*), post-Vedic Sanskrit and vernacular texts that encapsulate the twin Vedic goals of *Dharma* (righteous conduct) and *Brahman* / *Mokṣa* (spiritual liberation) while remaining open to all human beings—regardless of gender, *Varṇa* (including *Śūdras*), or Sanskrit literacy—claimed or were accorded the honorific status of **Pañcama Veda** ("Fifth Veda").

#### Exhaustive Inventory of All Texts Designated as the "Fifth Veda"

1. **Itihāsa-Purāṇa (The Earliest Upaniṣadic Attestation):**
   * *Primary Source:* **Chāndogya Upaniṣad 7.1.2** and **7.1.4** (*ṛgvedaṃ भगवोऽध्येमि यजुर्वेदं सामवेदमाथर्वणं चतुर्थमितिहासपुराणं पञ्चमं वेदानां वेदम्* — *itihāsapurāṇaṃ pañcamaṃ vedānām*). In this passage, Nārada tells Sanatkumāra: *"Venerable Sir, I have studied the Ṛgveda, the Yajurveda, the Sāmaveda, the Atharvaveda as the fourth, and Itihāsa-Purāṇa as the Fifth..."*
2. **The Mahābhārata (Including the Bhagavad Gītā):**
   * *Primary Source:* *Mahābhārata* (*Ādi Parva* 1.1.204, 1.56.33; *Svargārohaṇa Parva* 18.5.38). Explicitly styles itself the **Kārṣṇa Veda** (the Veda composed by Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana Vyāsa) and the *Pañcama Veda*, particularly because it also embeds an abbreviated *Rāmāyaṇa* (*Rāmopākhyāna* in the *Vana Parva*). It famously declares: *"Whatever is found here regarding Dharma, Artha, Kāma, and Mokṣa may be found elsewhere; whatever is not here is nowhere to be found"* (*Yad ihāsti tad anyatra, yan nehāsti na tat kvacit*).
3. **The Rāmāyaṇa of Vālmīki:**
   * Revered alongside the *Mahābhārata* as *Itihāsa* and explicitly praised in its opening chapters (*Bālakāṇḍa*) as equal to and embodying the Vedas in accessible epic poetry.
4. **The Purāṇas (Specifically the 18 *Mahāpurāṇas* and 18 *Upapurāṇas*, led by the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* and *Skanda Purāṇa*):**
   * *Primary Source:* *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.4.20 and 3.12.39 (*itihāsa-purāṇāni pañcamaṃ vedam īśvaraḥ / sarvebhya eva vaktrebhyaḥ sasṛje sarva-darśanaḥ* — stating that after creating the four Vedas from his four mouths, Brahmā created the *Itihāsas* and *Purāṇas* as the Fifth Veda from all four mouths combined), and the *Skanda Purāṇa* (*Revā Khaṇḍa* 1.18).
5. **The Nāṭyaśāstra (*Nāṭyaveda* of Bharata Muni):**
   * *Primary Source:* *Nāṭyaśāstra* Chapter 1, Verses 12–18. When the gods requested an audiovisual form of sacred instruction and recreation (*krīḍanīyakam*) accessible to all four *Varṇas* (including *Śūdras*, who were barred from hearing the four *Śruti* Vedas), Lord Brahmā created the **Nāṭyaveda** as the Fifth Veda by extracting four core components from the four Vedas:
     * **Pāṭhya** (Recitation / Spoken Words & Dialogue) from the **Ṛgveda**
     * **Gīta** (Music / Melodic Song) from the **Sāmaveda**
     * **Abhinaya** (Histrionic Expression / Gestures / Ritual Action) from the **Yajurveda**
     * **Rasa** (Aesthetic Sentiment / Emotional Essence) from the **Atharvaveda**
6. **Āyurveda (Specifically in the *Kāśyapa Saṃhitā* and *Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa*):**
   * Certain medical traditions elevate *Āyurveda* from an *Upaveda* to the *Pañcama Veda*, holding that physical and mental health is the indispensable foundation for fulfilling the four *Puruṣārthas* (*Dharma*, *Artha*, *Kāma*, *Mokṣa*).
7. **Tamil Vaiṣṇava Canon — The *Nālāyira Divya Prabandham* (Specifically Nammāḻvār's *Tiruvāymoḻi*):**
   * In the *Śrī Vaiṣṇava* tradition, which upholds **Ubhaya Vedānta** ("Dual Vedānta" giving equal canonical weight to Sanskrit and Tamil scriptures), the 4,000 Tamil hymns of the 12 *Āḻvārs* (compiled by Nāthamuni) are revered as the **Drāviḍa Veda** ("Tamil Veda"), with Nammāḻvār's 1,102-verse *Tiruvāymoḻi* hailed as the *Drāviḍa Veda Sāgaram* (correlated either to the *Sāmaveda* or the Fifth Veda) because it made the esoteric truths of the *Upaniṣads* accessible to all castes and genders.
8. **Tamil Śaiva Canon (*Tirumuṟai* / *Tevāram*) and the Ethical Canon (*Tirukkuṟaḷ*):**
   * In Tamil *Śaiva Siddhānta*, the 12 books of the **Tirumuṟai**—especially the **Tevāram** (Books 1–7, composed by Sambandar, Appar, and Sundarar) and Māṇikkavācakar's **Tiruvācakam** (Book 8)—are revered as the *Tamiḻ Maṟai* ("Tamil Veda") and Fifth Veda.
   * Similarly, Tiruvaḷḷuvar's classic ethical treatise **Tirukkuṟaḷ** (the *Kural*) is universally honored in Tamil tradition as the *Tamiḻ Maṟai* ("Tamil Veda"), *Deiva Nūl* ("Divine Book"), *Ulaga Podhu Maṟai* ("Universal Veda"), and *Drāviḍa Veda*.
9. **Tulsīdās's *Rāmcaritmānas* (16th-Century Awadhi/Hindi Bhakti Canon):**
   * Revered across North India as the living vernacular *Pañcama Veda* of the *Kali Yuga*, often placed alongside or above the Sanskrit Vedas in popular devotion.
10. **Kannada Vīraśaiva *Vacana Sāhitya*:**
    * The 12th-century Kannada prose-lyrics (*Vacanas*) composed by Basavaṇṇa, Allama Prabhu, Akka Mahādevī, and Cennabasavaṇṇa are revered in the Liṅgāyat tradition as a direct, egalitarian vernacular Veda.
11. **Kūṭiyāṭṭam and Kathakaḷi (Performative Temple Traditions of Kerala):**
    * In Kerala's *Cākyār Kūttu* and temple theater traditions, **Kūṭiyāṭṭam** (the living Sanskrit dramatic performance of the *Nāṭyaśāstra*) and **Kathakaḷi** are designated as the visual *Pañcama Veda*.

### X. Jātaveda (*Jātavedas* — The Ontological & Liturgical Fire Principle)

* **Reference:** [Jataveda — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jataveda)
* **Taxonomic Status Clarification:** **Jātaveda** (nominative singular: *Jātavedāḥ*; stem: *jātavedas-*, जातवेदस्) is **not a text, book, or literary layer**. It is a major Vedic Sanskrit epithet and manifestation of **Agni**, the Vedic god of fire. Specifically, *Jātaveda* designates Agni as the **terrestrial fire** and above all the **sacrificial altar fire**, who carries the oblations of the *Saṃhitās* and *Brāhmaṇas* from human worshippers to the *Devas*. In an extended theological sense, Agni-Jātaveda was understood as the divine instrument through whom worshippers attain knowledge of all existence, and as the **hypostasis of the inspiration behind the Vedas themselves**.
* **Five Classical Etymological Derivations:** Combining *jāta* ("born, brought into existence, created beings") and *veda* / *vedas* (from *vid-* "to know" or *vid-* "to find/possess," i.e., "knowledge, the Vedas, or wealth/possession"), Yāska's *Nirukta* (7.19) and the *Brāhmaṇas* (*Aitareya Brāhmaṇa* 3.36, *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* 9.5.1.68) explain *Jātavedas* in five distinct ways:
  1. *Jātāni veda*: **"Knowing all created beings"** (Omniscient witness of all generations across past, present, and future).
  2. *Jāte jāte vidyate* (or possessing all creatures): **"Possessing all creatures or everything existent"** / **"Present in every living being"** (as the internal digestive and vital fire, *Vaiśvānara* / *Jāṭharāgni*).
  3. *Jātāni enam viduḥ*: **"Known by created beings"** (Every living creature recognizes fire).
  4. *Jātavittaḥ* / *Jātadhanaḥ*: **"Possessing Vedas and riches"** (Acquiring all sacrificial wealth at birth).
  5. *Jātaprajñaḥ* / *Jātavedāḥ*: **"Possessing Vedas and wisdom"** (Innate wisdom at birth, or He from whom the Vedas were born).

#### Structural Role of *Jātaveda* Across the Vedic and Post-Vedic Corpus

1. **In the *Saṃhitās* (Dual Form of Agni: *Jātaveda* vs. *Kravyāda*):**
   * Agni opens the *Ṛgveda* (1.1.1), and the epithet *Jātavedas* occurs over 120 times in the *Ṛgveda Saṃhitā* (e.g., RV 1.44.1, 1.99.1, 3.1.20, 4.3.8, 10.15.12, 10.16.1–9).
   * In **Ṛgveda 10.16** (the Funeral Hymn), Agni is strictly bifurcated into two ritual forms:
     * **Jātaveda** (*Havyavāhana* / *Kavyavāhana*): The pure, auspicious sacrificial altar fire that knows the immortal realms, carries oblations (*Havis*) to the *Devas* and *Pitṛs* (ancestors), and conveys the purified spirit of the deceased to the world of the forefathers (*Pitṛloka*).
     * **Kravyāda** ("Flesh-Eater"): The fierce funeral pyre fire that consumes the physical corpse and must be ritually dismissed before *Jātaveda* is invoked for auspicious sacrifices.
2. **In the *Durgā Sūktam* & *Ṛgveda* 1.99.1 (Kaśyapa's Hymn):**
   * The single-verse hymn **Ṛgveda 1.99.1** (*Jātavedase sunavāma somam arātīyato nidahāti vedaḥ / sa naḥ parṣad ati durgāṇi viśvā nāveva sindhuṃ duritāty agniḥ* — *"For Jātaveda let us press the Soma; may He burn down the wealth of the enemy; may He, Agni, ferry us across all difficulties [Durgāṇi] like a boat across a river"*) forms the opening verse of the **Durgā Sūktam** (embedded in *Taittirīya Āraṇyaka* 10.1.7 / *Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad*), linking Vedic *Jātaveda* directly with the fiery saving consciousness of Goddess Durgā.
3. **In the *Śrī Sūktam* (*Ṛgveda Khila* 5.87):**
   * Throughout the 15 core verses of the **Śrī Sūktam** (*Hiraṇyavarṇāṃ hariṇīṃ... tāṃ ma āvaha jātavedo lakṣmīm anapagāminīm*), the priest repeatedly invokes *Jātaveda* as the divine mediator capable of bringing Goddess Mahālakṣmī (*Śrī*) to the worshipper.
4. **In the *Upaniṣads* (*Kena Upaniṣad* 3.1–3.6 — The *Yakṣa* Allegory):**
   * When *Brahman* appears before the victorious Vedic gods as a mysterious Spirit (*Yakṣa*), the gods send Agni first to discover its identity, addressing him specifically as **Jātaveda** (*"O Jātaveda, find out what this great Spirit is!"*). When Agni boasts that he can burn everything on earth, *Brahman* places a single blade of grass before him; Agni (*Jātaveda*) is unable to burn it, illustrating that even the omniscient Vedic sacrificial fire derives its power entirely from *Brahman* (marking the philosophical transition from *Saṃhitā*/*Brāhmaṇa* ritualism to Upaniṣadic *Vedānta*).
5. **In Post-Vedic Epic and Purāṇic Literature (*Śiva*, *Viṣṇu*, and the *Brahma Purāṇa* Myth):**
   * In post-Vedic literature, *Jātaveda* is also applied as a direct epithet of **Śiva** (and **Viṣṇu**) in their cosmic fiery aspects.
   * The **Brahma Purāṇa** (p. 171) preserves an etiological myth explaining the title: *Jātaveda* was originally the elder brother of Agni who delivered sacrificial offerings (*Haviṣya*) to the gods until he was slain by the demon **Madhu** (son of Diti), after which the *Devas* and *Ṛṣis* appointed Agni to assume the title and office of the new *Jātaveda*.

## 5. Canonical Enumeration of the 18 *Mahāpurāṇas* and 18 *Upapurāṇas* (Under the "Fifth Veda" Claim)

Because the *Purāṇas* (led by the *Bhāgavata* and *Skanda Purāṇas*, alongside *Chāndogya Upaniṣad* 7.1.2) claim the status of the **Fifth Veda** (*Pañcama Veda*) and are traditionally divided into **18 Mahāpurāṇas** (Great Purāṇas) and **18 Upapurāṇas** (Minor Purāṇas), their canonical lists are recorded below:

* **The 18 Mahāpurāṇas** (memorized via the classical mnemonic verse *Madvayaṃ bhadvayaṃ caiva brattrayaṃ vacatuṣṭayam / anāpaliṅgakūskāni purāṇāni pṛthak pṛthak*):
  1. **M (2):** *Matsya Purāṇa*, *Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa* (which embeds the *Devī Māhātmya* / *Durgā Saptaśatī*)
  2. **Bh (2):** *Bhaviṣya Purāṇa*, *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (*Śrīmad Bhāgavatam*)
  3. **Br (3):** *Brahma Purāṇa* (*Ādi Purāṇa*), *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa* (which embeds the *Lalitā Sahasranāma* and *Adhyātma Rāmāyaṇa*), *Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa*
  4. **V (4):** *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*, *Vāyu Purāṇa* (sometimes alternated with the *Śiva Mahāpurāṇa*), *Vāmana Purāṇa*, *Varāha Purāṇa*
  5. **A (1):** *Agni Purāṇa* (encyclopedic compendium including *Dhanurveda*, poetics, and ritual)
  6. **Nā (1):** *Nārada Purāṇa* (*Nāradīya Purāṇa*)
  7. **Pa (1):** *Padma Purāṇa*
  8. **Liṅga (1):** *Liṅga Purāṇa*
  9. **Kū (1):** *Kūrma Purāṇa*
  10. **Skā (1):** *Skanda Purāṇa* (the largest *Mahāpurāṇa*, containing 81,000 verses)
  11. **Ga (1):** *Garuḍa Purāṇa*
* **The 18 Principal Upapurāṇas** (per the *Kūrma Purāṇa* and *Devībhāgavata Purāṇa* lists):
  1. *Sanatkumāra*, 2. *Nārasiṃha* (*Nṛsiṃha*), 3. *Bṛhannāradīya*, 4. *Śivarahasya* (or *Śivadharma*), 5. *Durvāsā*, 6. *Kāpila*, 7. *Vāmana* (or *Mānava*), 8. *Bhārgava*, 9. *Vāruṇa*, 10. *Kālikā*, 11. *Sāmba*, 12. *Nandi*, 13. *Saura* (or *Sūrya*), 14. *Pārāśara*, 15. *Āditya*, 16. *Māheśvara*, 17. *Bhāgavata* (*Devībhāgavata Purāṇa* — claimed by *Śāktas* as a *Mahāpurāṇa*), and 18. *Vāsiṣṭha* (or *Gaṇeśa* and *Mudgala Purāṇas* in *Gāṇapatya* canons).

## 7. Glossary of Core Sanskrit and Vedic Terminology

* **Adhvaryu (अध्वर्यु):** The Yajurvedic executor priest responsible for the physical actions of the sacrifice—measuring the sacrificial ground, building the fire altar, preparing vessels, and pouring oblations while reciting *Yajus* formulas.
* **Anukramaṇī (अनुक्रमणी):** Systematic canonical index recording the seer (*Ṛṣi*), deity (*Devatā*), meter (*Chandas*), opening word (*pratīka*), and verse count of Vedic hymns.
* **Apauruṣeya (अपौरुषेय):** "Not of human (or personal) agency"; the orthodox *Mīmāṃsā* and *Vedānta* doctrine that the *Śruti* Vedas are eternal, unauthored revelations apprehended by *Ṛṣis*.
* **Āraṇyaka (आरण्यक):** "Wilderness or Forest Treatise"; the third internal layer of each Veda, interpreting complex rituals (*Mahāvrata*, *Pravargya*) through esoteric symbolism and internal meditation (*Upāsanā*).
* **Arthavāda (अर्थवाद):** Explanatory, mythological, and eulogistic prose passages in the *Brāhmaṇas* that motivate and praise the performance of ritual injunctions (*Vidhi*).
* **Āśrama (आश्रम):** The four classical stages of human life in Hinduism: *Brahmacarya* (celibate studentship), *Gṛhastha* (householder), *Vānaprastha* (forest hermit), and *Sannyāsa* (renunciate).
* **Ātman (आत्मन्):** The innermost Self, individual soul, or pure witness-consciousness, whose relationship or identity with *Brahman* forms the central inquiry of the *Upaniṣads* and *Vedānta*.
* **Brahman (ब्रह्मन्):** (1) Neuter (*Bráhman*): In the *Saṃhitās* and *Brāhmaṇas*, the sacred Vedic prayer or liturgical power; in the *Upaniṣads* and *Vedānta*, the Ultimate, Infinite, Unchanging Reality and ground of all existence. (2) Masculine (*Brahmán*): The supervising priest of the *Atharvaveda* who silently oversees the entire *Yajña* and repairs ritual errors.
* **Brāhmaṇa (ब्राह्मण):** The second internal layer of each Veda, consisting of Vedic Sanskrit prose commentaries that explain the meaning of the *Saṃhitā* mantras and prescribe the rules (*Vidhi*) and rationales (*Arthavāda*) for performing *Yajña* sacrifices.
* **Caraṇavyūha (चरणव्यूह):** An important *Pariśiṣṭa* treatise (preserved in both a *Śukla Yajurvedic* recension attributed to Kātyāyana and an *Atharvavedic* recension) that catalogs the schools (*Śākhās*), textual divisions, verse counts, and *Upavedas* of the four Vedas.
* **Caturveda (चतुर्वेद):** The collective designation for the four canonical Vedas (*Ṛgveda*, *Yajurveda*, *Sāmaveda*, *Atharvaveda*).
* **Hotṛ (होतृ):** The Ṛgvedic priest who presides over the recitation of metrical praise hymns (*Śastras* / *Ṛcs*) to invoke the *Devas* to the sacrificial altar.
* **Itihāsa (इतिहास):** "Thus indeed it was"; epic history comprising the *Mahābhārata* and the *Rāmāyaṇa*, coupled with *Purāṇa* in *Chāndogya Upaniṣad* 7.1.2 as the Fifth Veda.
* **Jātaveda / Jātavedas (जातवेदस्):** Preeminent Vedic epithet of **Agni** as the terrestrial sacrificial altar fire—"He who knows all born creatures" or "He who possesses the Vedas and wisdom."
* **Jñāna-Kāṇḍa (ज्ञानकाण्ड):** The "Knowledge Portion" (*Uttara-kāṇḍa*) of the Vedas, comprising the *Upaniṣads* (and often the *Āraṇyakas*), focused on self-realization and *Mokṣa*.
* **Karma-Kāṇḍa (कर्मकाण्ड):** The "Action/Ritual Portion" (*Pūrva-kāṇḍa*) of the Vedas, comprising the *Saṃhitās* and *Brāhmaṇas*, focused on sacrificial rites (*Yajña*) and *Dharma*.
* **Khila / Khila-sūkta (खिलसूक्त):** Supplementary or apocryphal hymns appended to a canonical *Saṃhitā* (most notably the 36 *Khilas* of the *Bāṣkala* recension of the *Ṛgveda*, which include the *Śrī Sūktam*).
* **Mahāvākya (महावाक्य):** "Great Saying"; the four foundational Upaniṣadic aphorisms (*Prajñānaṃ Brahma*, *Ahaṃ Brahmāsmi*, *Tat Tvam Asi*, *Ayam Ātmā Brahma*) expressing the unity of *Ātman* and *Brahman*.
* **Mīmāṃsā (मीमांसा):** "Revered Inquiry" or critical exegesis of the Vedas, divided into **Pūrva Mīmāṃsā** (Jaimini's inquiry into ritual *Dharma* in the *Karma-Kāṇḍa*) and **Uttara Mīmāṃsā** (Bādarāyaṇa's inquiry into *Brahman* in the *Jñāna-Kāṇḍa*, i.e., *Vedānta*).
* **Mukhya Upaniṣads (मुख्य उपनिषद्):** The 10 to 13 oldest, canonical Principal *Upaniṣads* rooted directly in Vedic *Śākhās* and commented upon by the founders of the Vedānta schools.
* **Muktikā Canon (मुक्तिका):** The canonical list of **108 Upaniṣads** codified in the *Muktikā Upaniṣad* (grouped into 10 RV, 19 SYV, 32 KYV, 16 SV, and 31 AV texts).
* **Pañcama Veda (पञ्चम वेद):** "Fifth Veda"; an honorific title of supreme spiritual and cultural authority bestowed upon post-Vedic Sanskrit and vernacular works (*Itihāsa-Purāṇa*, *Mahābhārata*, *Nāṭyaśāstra*, *Āyurveda*, *Rāmcaritmānas*, *Tiruvāymoḻi*, *Tevāram*, *Tirukkuṟaḷ*) that make Vedic truth accessible to all people.
* **Pariśiṣṭa (परिशिष्ट):** "Supplement, remainder, or appendix"; late-Vedic ancillary treatises attached to each Veda and its *Kalpasūtras* that elaborate on ritual minutiae, domestic rites, astrology, and omens.
* **Pāṭha (पाठ):** Formal mnemonic modes of oral Vedic recitation (3 simple *Prakṛti-pāṭhas* and 8 complex *Vikṛti-pāṭhas* culminating in *Ghana-pāṭha*) designed to preserve phonetic and word order accuracy without a single syllable of alteration.
* **Prasthānatrayī (प्रस्थानत्रयी):** The "Threefold Canon" of *Vedānta* philosophy: the *Upaniṣads* (*Śruti-prasthāna*), the *Bhagavad Gītā* (*Smṛti-prasthāna*), and the *Brahma Sūtras* (*Nyāya-prasthāna*).
* **Ṛc (ऋच्):** A metrical Vedic verse of praise (plural: *Ṛcaḥ*), collected in the *Ṛgveda Saṃhitā*.
* **Śākhā (शाखा):** Literally "branch" or "limb"; a traditional theological school and textual recension transmitting its own specific version of a Veda's *Saṃhitā*, *Brāhmaṇa*, *Āraṇyaka*, *Upaniṣad*, and *Kalpasūtras*.
* **Sāman (सामन्):** A Vedic verse (almost always drawn from the *Ṛgveda*) set to a liturgical musical melody and chanted by the *Udgātṛ* priest during Soma sacrifices.
* **Saṃhitā (संहिता):** "Joined together, collection"; the oldest, foundational layer of each Veda containing the *Mantras* joined according to euphonic *sandhi* rules; also used as a title for post-Vedic compendia.
* **Smṛti (स्मृति):** "That which is remembered"; sacred literature composed by human sages (including the *Vedāṅgas*, *Sūtras*, *Upavedas*, *Itihāsas*, *Purāṇas*, and *Dharmaśāstras*) that derives its authority from *Śruti*.
* **Śruti (श्रुति):** "That which is heard"; the primary revealed canon of Hinduism, consisting strictly of the four Vedas and their four internal layers (*Saṃhitā*, *Brāhmaṇa*, *Āraṇyaka*, *Upaniṣad*).
* **Trayī Vidyā (त्रयी विद्या):** The "Threefold Knowledge" comprising the *Ṛgveda* (verses), *Yajurveda* (sacrificial prose formulas), and *Sāmaveda* (chants).
* **Udgātṛ (उद्गातृ):** The Sāmavedic priest who sings the *Sāman* melodies (*Stotras*) during Soma sacrifices.
* **Upaniṣad (उपनिषद्):** "Sitting down near [a teacher]"; the fourth and final layer of each Veda (*Jñāna-Kāṇḍa* / *Vedānta*), exploring metaphysics, consciousness, meditation, and the nature of *Ātman* and *Brahman*.
* **Upāsanā-Kāṇḍa (उपासनाकाण्ड):** The transitional Vedic sphere of interiorized meditation and symbolic worship centered in the *Āraṇyakas* and devotional sections of the *Brāhmaṇas* and *Upaniṣads*.
* **Upaveda (उपवेद):** "Applied Knowledge" or "Sub-Veda"; the four technical sciences (*Āyurveda*, *Dhanurveda*, *Gāndharvaveda*, and *Sthāpatyaveda*/*Śilpaśāstra* or *Arthaśāstra*) affiliated with the four Vedas.
* **Vedāṅga (वेदाङ्ग):** "Limbs of the Veda"; the six auxiliary disciplines (*Śikṣā*, *Chandas*, *Vyākaraṇa*, *Nirukta*, *Jyotiṣa*, *Kalpa*) required for the preservation, linguistic analysis, and ritual application of the Vedas.
* **Vedānta (वेदान्त):** "The End or Essence of the Vedas"; denotes both the *Upaniṣads* as the concluding layer of the Vedic corpus and the orthodox philosophical schools (*Uttara Mīmāṃsā*) founded upon the *Prasthānatrayī*.
* **Vidhi (विधि):** A mandatory ritual injunction or directive in the *Brāhmaṇas* prescribing a sacrificial action.
* **Yajus (यजुस्):** A sacrificial formula (typically in rhythmic Vedic prose) muttered by the *Adhvaryu* priest while performing ritual actions, collected in the *Yajurveda*.
